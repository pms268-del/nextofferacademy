## A. Opportunities (40%)

**A1 [job claim]** Quick reality check on AI training gigs: the legit ones (DataAnnotation, Outlier, Prolific, TELUS) do pay real people. But "accepted" and "working" are two different things. Outlier workers call it the empty queue. Apply to 3 at once and keep your regular search running.
*Source: theairankings.com Outlier review; Indeed DataAnnotation reviews*

**A2 [job claim]** If you were laid off from a specialist role (accounting, nursing, law, engineering, teaching), look at expert AI-training listings. Mercor has listed $50–140/hr expert roles. Contract work, not a salary, but it pays for what you already know.
*Source: work.mercor.com Expert Model Trainer listing*

**A3 [job claim]** US grad students: Handshake's AI Fellowship lists $50–125/hr for master's and PhD students and recent grads, in any field. Applying doesn't guarantee a project, and projects can pause, but your career office probably already promotes it.
*Source: Handshake EDU-partner FAQ; Cornell Graduate School posting*

**A4 [job claim]** Prolific has a rule a lot of survey sites don't: researchers must pay at least $8/hr, and Prolific recommends $12. Not life-changing money, but it's a floor. New sign-ups may hit a waitlist.
*Source: Prolific researcher help center*

**A5 [job claim]** Remote job with a W-2 paycheck? Apple, Amazon, Concentrix and TTEC all hire remote support reps. Pay and eligible states vary. Apply ONLY on their official careers sites. Fake recruiters copy all four.
*Source: tech.co; third-party pay reports (see playbook OD039–OD042)*

**A6 [job claim]** Developers: AI companies pay for code evaluation. Turing's US recruiting page advertises $50–250/hr projects (10–20 hrs/week typical) after coding tests and an AI interview. Matches aren't guaranteed, so prep for timed tests.
*Source: turing.hackerearth.com*

**A7 [job claim]** Professionals between jobs: expert networks like GLG pay for 1-hour calls about your industry. Rates vary widely and invites are irregular. And never share confidential info from a current or past employer.
*Source: expertopportunities.com; arbolus.com*

**A8 [job claim]** Paid research interviews are underrated for professionals. Respondent studies have been reported at $50–400+ each. You won't qualify for most, but filling out your profile accurately is 10 minutes well spent.
*Source: finder.com*

**A9** Free remote job boards worth checking daily: We Work Remotely (has a USA-only filter), Remotive, Himalayas, Wellfound (shows salary + equity), and YC's Work at a Startup. FlexJobs charges; try the free ones first.
*Source: playbook directory OD061–OD063, OD021–OD022, OD069*

**A10** Laid off this year? Two free things to do this week: file for unemployment in the state where you worked, and book an American Job Center appointment (there are about 2,300). Both cost nothing, and most people skip the second.
*Source: dol.gov American Job Centers; CareerOneStop*

**A11 [job claim]** Tutoring online? Wyzant lets you set your rate. Its commission has been reported at 25% until you've earned $2,000, then 20%. Assigned-session sites like Varsity Tutors pay less but find students for you.
*Source: theinterviewguys.com; sidehusl.com*

**A12** YC's Work at a Startup works differently from job boards: you fill out one profile, and founders of YC-funded startups reach out to you. You can still apply to specific companies too.
*Source: workatastartup.com/faq*

## B. Applications, CV & interviews (30%)

**B1** The most common way people fail AI training entry tests isn't lack of knowledge. It's missing an instruction. "Under 100 words." "Explain why." "Use bullet points." Read the prompt twice before you write a word.

**B2** AI interviewers (Mercor, Alignerr's "Zara", micro1) build their questions from your resume. Before you start, practice explaining your 3 biggest projects in 2 minutes each: what you did, how, and the result with a number.
*Source: Mercor AI interview docs; Alignerr Trustpilot reviews*

**B3** Unpopular opinion: the "75% of resumes get auto-rejected by ATS" stat gets repeated way more than it's backed up. The real killers are tables, columns, and contact info stuck in the header. Simple single-column resume > fancy template.
*Source: atsverification.com; resumegenius.com*

**B4** 30-second resume test: open your PDF, select all, paste into a plain-text editor. If the text comes out scrambled, an applicant tracking system may read it scrambled too.

**B5** When you use AI on your resume, add this line to the prompt: "Use only my facts. Never invent numbers. Write [ADD METRIC?] where a number is missing." Then fill in the real numbers yourself.

**B6** Upwork proposal tip: the client sees your first line or two in the preview. Don't spend them on "Dear Sir/Madam, I'm a hardworking professional." Restate THEIR goal in your words.

**B7** The best message to a hiring manager has 3 parts: one specific detail about them or their team, one sentence about why you're relevant, and one easy ask. That's it.

**B8** Laid off and dreading "why did you leave?" Try: "My role was one of [X] eliminated in a restructuring. I'm proud of [one result]. Now I'm focused on [what this job needs]." Factual, short, no bitterness.

**B9** Freelance pricing formula: quote = (your target hourly × hours) ÷ (1 − platform fee). Fiverr keeps 20%, so a $210 job at your rate should be quoted around $263. Then set aside ~25–30% for taxes.
*Source: Fiverr fee guides (vaultleap.com)*

**B10** Customer service interview: when a customer is furious, don't argue and don't over-apologize. Acknowledge the specific problem, say what you're doing right now, offer two concrete options. Interviewers listen for that sequence.

## C. Worker experiences & insights (20%)

**C1** What first-client timelines on Upwork actually look like, from freelancers on Upwork's community forum: "about 2 months to land my first client, and 3 weeks after that." Others sent proposals for 6 months and broke through only after narrowing their niche.
*Source: community.upwork.com*

**C2 [job claim]** Business Insider reported an Outlier annotator whose pay on generalist projects dropped from $50 to $15 an hour overnight. The lesson for AI gig work: specialize where you can, and never depend on one platform.
*Source: Business Insider via Yahoo*

**C3** Why remote jobs feel impossible to land: LinkedIn data showed remote roles were 8.4% of postings but drew 42% of applications. That's why applying in the first 24–48 hours and messaging someone on the team matters so much.
*Source: Sherwood News (LinkedIn data)*

**C4** In one dataset of 461,000 tracked applications (Q2 2025), the median time to a first offer was 68.5 days. If you're at week 4 with nothing, that's normal. Adjust your approach, but don't assume you're failing.
*Source: Huntr Q2 2025*

**C5** FTC data: "task scam" reports went from about 5,000 in all of 2023 to about 20,000 in the first half of 2024. If a "recruiter" texts you about rating apps for money, it's a scam. Legit jobs pay you; you never pay them.
*Source: FTC press release, Dec 2024*

**C6** DataAnnotation sits at 4.1/5 on Indeed with reviewers calling pay "prompt and problem free". The most common complaints are about getting in (one-shot test, silent rejections) and dry spells, not about payment.
*Source: Indeed reviews (June 2026)*

**C7** xAI laid off at least 500 generalist AI tutors in Sept 2025 while saying it would grow its specialist team 10x. If you're doing AI training work, building a specialty is the safest move.
*Source: Business Insider via Reuters*

**C8** Before taking a "work from home" call-center gig, ask: W-2 or 1099? Liveops says agents pay a $25 background check, certification is unpaid, and some programs pay 25–34¢ per talk minute. Do the math on your real hourly rate.
*Source: join.liveops.com/faqs*
