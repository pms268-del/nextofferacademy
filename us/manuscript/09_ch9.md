# AI-Powered Resume & Application Workflow {#ch9}

> **In this chapter:** a repeatable 45-minute workflow that takes one real job posting to a tailored, truthful, ATS-readable resume, a LinkedIn or freelance profile, a cover note, and an application-ready PDF. All of it uses free tools: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Google Docs, or Microsoft Word. The full prompt library is in `AI_CAREER_PROMPTS.md`, and the key prompts are reproduced here.

## The non-negotiable rule: never invent

AI assistants will happily make up a certification, a metric, or a job title that "sounds right." Every prompt in this book tells the AI not to, and uses placeholders such as **[ADD METRIC?]** instead of guesses. You must still check every line. Invented qualifications can surface in background checks, reference calls, interviews, or a platform's domain assessment, and on AI-training platforms the expertise you claim is the expertise you will be tested on.

## What ATS systems actually do (2026)

- Most applicant tracking systems **parse** your resume into fields and let recruiters **search and filter** by keywords. Reports that "75% of resumes are auto-rejected by ATS" are widely described as overblown. Many systems do not auto-reject on content. Knockout questions (work authorization, location, required license) are the more common automatic filters.
- **Parsing problems are real.** Contact details placed in headers or footers, tables, text boxes, multiple columns, and images can be misread. A simple single-column document parses best.
- **Text-based PDFs are fine** for most modern systems. Submit a .docx when the employer asks for it.
- **Keywords still matter** because recruiters search for them. Mirror the posting's exact phrasing *where it truthfully describes you* ("customer success" vs. "client support," "SQL" vs. "databases").

## The 45-minute workflow

::: {.flow}
1. **Decode the job (5 min).** Paste the full posting into prompt P01. Save the must-haves and keywords.
2. **Check fit honestly (5 min).** P02 compares your resume to the job. Skip jobs below about 6/10 unless you have a referral.
3. **Tailor the resume (15 min).** P03 rewrites it with the job's keywords, using only your facts. Answer P04's questions to add real metrics.
4. **Write the note (5 min).** P11 produces a cover letter under 200 words (or P12 for an Upwork proposal).
5. **QA and export (10 min).** P21 checks consistency. Paste into the template below, export to PDF, and check how it reads.
6. **Log it (5 min).** Add the application to your Workbook tracker with a follow-up date 5–7 days out.
:::

## Before and after: a real-world style rewrite

::: {.beforeafter}
**Target posting (summary):** *Remote Customer Success Associate, B2B SaaS. Must-haves: 2+ yrs customer-facing experience, Zendesk or similar, ability to manage 50+ accounts, strong written communication. Nice: SQL basics, onboarding experience.*

**Before**

> **Retail Store Supervisor**, Target, 2021–2025
>
> - Responsible for helping customers
> - Managed team members
> - Handled returns and complaints
> - Did inventory

**After** (using only facts the candidate confirmed via P04)

> **Retail Store Supervisor**, Target · Austin, TX · Jun 2021 – Mar 2025
>
> - Resolved 40–60 customer issues per shift (returns, order problems, escalations) while maintaining store guest-satisfaction scores above district average.
> - Trained and scheduled a team of 9; onboarded 14 new hires using a checklist I created, cutting ramp-up time from about 3 weeks to 2.
> - Managed the store's online-order pickup queue (~120 orders/day) in the company's order-management system; flagged recurring errors to regional support.
> - Wrote weekly shift summaries for the store manager covering incidents, customer feedback themes, and staffing gaps.

**What changed:** ownership verbs, real volumes, the posting's language ("customer issues," "escalations," "onboarded"), and a written-communication proof point. **What did not change:** the title, the employer, and the facts. Zendesk was *not* added because the candidate has not used it; it goes in the cover note as "quick to learn new ticketing tools, e.g., our order-management system."
:::

## ATS-friendly resume template (copy into Google Docs or Word)

```
FIRST LAST
City, ST · email@domain.com · linkedin.com/in/yourname · (portfolio link, optional)

SUMMARY
[Target title] with [X years] in [domain]. [Top proof point with number]. [Second proof point]. Seeking [remote role type].

SKILLS
[8–14 skills/tools, mirroring the posting's exact wording where true]

EXPERIENCE
Job Title, Employer · City, ST (or Remote) · Mon YYYY – Mon YYYY
- Verb + what you did + tool/method + measurable result.
- (4–6 bullets for the most relevant role; 2–3 for older roles)

PROJECTS (optional; great for new grads and career switchers)
Project Name · Tools · Mon YYYY
- What it does, who it's for, the result or link.

EDUCATION
Degree, Major · School · Year  (GPA if 3.3+ and within 3 years of graduating)

CERTIFICATIONS (only real, current ones)
```

**Formatting rules:** a standard font (Calibri, Arial, Garamond) at 10.5–12 pt · 0.5–1" margins · no tables, columns, icons, or photos · contact info in the body, not the header · consistent dates · one page (two if you have more than 10 years of relevant experience).

## Exporting application-ready PDFs

1. **Google Docs:** File → Download → PDF Document (.pdf). **Word:** File → Save As → PDF (choose "Best for electronic distribution and accessibility").
2. **Name the file** `First-Last-Resume-[Role].pdf` (e.g., `Jordan-Lee-Resume-Customer-Success.pdf`).
3. **Test it:** open the PDF, press Ctrl/Cmd+A, copy, and paste into a plain-text editor. If the text comes out in order with your contact line at the top, it parses well. If it's scrambled, remove columns and tables.
4. **Keep a master resume** (everything you've done) and save each tailored version in a folder per application.
5. **Design tools** (Canva and similar) produce attractive resumes, but templates with columns and graphics can parse poorly. Use them for a portfolio, not for the resume you submit to an ATS.
