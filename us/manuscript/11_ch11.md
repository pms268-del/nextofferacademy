# LinkedIn & Networking That Leads to Real Openings {#ch11}

> **In this chapter:** how to find the recruiters, hiring managers, alumni, founders, and employees connected to the jobs you want; how to reach out, hold coffee chats, and ask for referrals without being awkward; and how to follow up. It includes 17 copy-ready templates and a 7-day networking plan. Remote roles are heavily contested (8.4% of LinkedIn postings drew 42% of applications), and the documented successes in Chapter 6 lean on networks and recruiter contact.

## Step 1: Make your profile searchable (60 minutes, once)

Run prompt **P08**, then check:

- [ ] **Headline** names the target role and specialty, not just "Seeking opportunities." *Example:* "Customer Success & Onboarding | B2B SaaS | Zendesk, HubSpot | Open to remote roles"
- [ ] **Open to Work** is set. Choose *recruiters only* (visible to people using LinkedIn Recruiter) if you prefer privacy, or the public green banner. LinkedIn says it tries to hide the signal from your current employer but cannot guarantee it.
- [ ] **About** opens with your value in two lines and includes 8–10 keywords.
- [ ] **Experience** bullets match your resume, with numbers.
- [ ] **Skills**: the top 10 skills recruiters search for in your target role.
- [ ] **Location** is your real city or metro. Many remote roles are limited to certain states.
- [ ] A **professional photo** and a custom URL (linkedin.com/in/firstlast).

## Step 2: Find the right people

### For a specific job posting

1. Open the posting. Look for **"Meet the hiring team"** or the person who posted it.
2. Go to the company page → **People** → search the team name ("customer success," "data").
3. Identify: **(a)** the likely hiring manager (a team lead or manager title in that function), **(b)** the recruiter (a "Talent Acquisition," "Recruiter," or "People" title), and **(c)** 1–2 people currently in the role.

### Alumni (one of the warmest cold contacts)

Go to your school's LinkedIn page → **Alumni** (linkedin.com/school/[your-school]/people/). Filter by **where they work**, **what they do**, and **where they live**. Alumni at target companies are among the most likely strangers to answer you.

### LinkedIn search operators (people search)

| Goal | Search string |
|---|---|
| Recruiters at a company | `"technical recruiter" OR "talent acquisition"` + Current company filter |
| Hiring managers for a function | `("customer success manager" OR "head of support") AND remote` |
| People in your target role | `"AI trainer" OR "data annotator" OR "RLHF"` |
| Founders of AI startups | `founder AND ("YC" OR "Y Combinator") AND AI` |
| Exclude noise | `recruiter NOT "staffing"` |

LinkedIn supports quotation marks, AND, OR, NOT, and parentheses in search. Combine them with the **Current company**, **Location**, and **School** filters.

### Google "X-ray" search (works without LinkedIn filters)

```
site:linkedin.com/in "recruiter" "Company Name"
site:linkedin.com/in "customer success" "Company Name" "University of X"
```

### Founders and startup teams

- **YC Work at a Startup** and **Wellfound** list founders by name on company pages.
- Founders often post hiring needs on LinkedIn and X. Search "we're hiring" plus your role, and set the date filter to "past week."

## Step 3: Reach out (the 3-part message)

Every outreach message has **one specific detail** (why them), **one sentence about you** (why it matters to them), and **one easy ask** (yes/no, 15 minutes, or "who should I talk to?").

> **Note on limits:** free LinkedIn accounts may cap how many personalized connection notes you can send each month. If you hit the cap, connect without a note and send your message once they accept, or reach out by email if their address is public.

## 17 outreach templates

*Replace every [bracket]. Keep connection notes under 300 characters. Personalize the first line every time; a template that looks like a template gets ignored.*

::: {.template}
**T1: Recruiter, specific opening (connection note)**

> Hi [Name], I just applied for the [Role] (job ID [#]) on your team. I've spent [X years] doing [closest relevant work], most recently [one result]. Happy to share anything that helps your review. Thanks!

**T2: Recruiter, follow-up after connecting**

> Thanks for connecting, [Name]. For the [Role] opening: the posting emphasizes [must-have]. In my last role I [proof point with number]. My resume is in the system under [email]. Is there anything else useful for me to know about the process or timeline?

**T3: Hiring manager, applied already**

> Hi [Name], I saw you're hiring a [Role]. I applied today. I noticed your team [specific detail: launched X / posted about Y]. I've done similar work: [one-line result]. If it's helpful, I'd welcome 15 minutes to learn what success looks like in the first 90 days.

**T4: Hiring manager, no posting yet**

> Hi [Name], I've followed [Company]'s work on [specific]. I'm a [role] with experience in [skill] and I'm exploring remote roles on teams like yours. Are you expecting to grow the team this year? If so, I'd love to stay on your radar.

**T5: Employee in the target role (informational chat)**

> Hi [Name], I'm exploring [Role] roles and your path from [their past role] to [current] caught my eye. Would you be open to a 15-minute chat about what the work is like at [Company]? Happy to work around your schedule.

**T6: Alumni**

> Hi [Name], fellow [School] alum ([your year/major]) here. I'm moving into [field] and saw you're at [Company]. Would you have 15 minutes in the next couple of weeks to share how you got started? I'd really appreciate it.

**T7: Former colleague, reconnecting after a layoff**

> Hi [Name], it's been a while since [shared project]! Quick update: my role at [Company] was eliminated in a restructuring, and I'm looking for [target role], ideally remote. If you hear of anything, or know someone I should talk to, I'd be grateful. How are things with you?

**T8: Asking for a referral (after a conversation)**

> Thanks again for the chat last week, [Name]. It confirmed [Company] is where I'd like to be. There's an open [Role] (link) that matches what we discussed about [team need]. Would you be comfortable referring me? I've attached a 3-line summary you can paste, and no pressure at all if it's not a fit.
>
> *Summary:* [Name], [X years] in [skill]; [result 1]; [result 2]; applying for [Role/ID].

**T9: Startup founder**

> Hi [Name], I've been using/reading about [Product] and [specific observation]. I'm a [role] who has [relevant result]. If you're hiring for [function], I'd love to help. Here's a 1-page idea for [small improvement]: [link]. Open to a quick call?

**T10: AI-training / expert work outreach (to an AI startup's ops or recruiting lead)**

> Hi [Name], I'm a [credential, e.g., licensed CPA / PhD in molecular biology] with [X years] in [sub-field]. I've seen [Company] recruiting domain experts for model evaluation. I'd be glad to help on [domain] projects. I applied via [portal] on [date]. Is there a specific listing I should target?

**T11: Freelance prospect (cold, with a specific fix)**

> Hi [Name], I noticed [specific issue: your booking link on the Services page returns an error / your newsletter signup isn't connected]. I fix things like this for [type of business]. Here's a 2-minute video showing the fix: [link]. Want me to handle it this week for $[X]?

**T12: Former client or manager, asking for a testimonial or recommendation**

> Hi [Name], I'm building my freelance practice in [service]. Would you be willing to write 2–3 sentences about the [project] we did together for my LinkedIn or Upwork profile? I can draft something for you to edit if that's easier.

**T13: Thank-you after a coffee chat (send within 24 hours)**

> Thank you for your time today, [Name]. Your point about [specific insight] was especially helpful, and I'm going to [action you'll take]. I'll keep you posted on how it goes.

**T14: Follow-up, no response (5–7 days later)**

> Hi [Name], just bumping this in case it got buried. Totally understand if now isn't a good time. If someone else on the team is better to ask about [Role/topic], I'd appreciate a pointer.

**T15: Follow-up after interview (24 hours)**

> Thank you for speaking with me about the [Role], [Name]. I enjoyed discussing [topic]. It reinforced my interest, particularly [specific]. One thing I didn't mention: [brief extra proof point]. I look forward to next steps.

**T16: Status check after interview (if past the promised date)**

> Hi [Name], I hope your week is going well. I wanted to check on the timeline for the [Role]. I'm still very interested, and happy to provide anything else you need.

**T17: Staying in touch after a rejection**

> Thank you for letting me know, [Name]. I appreciated learning about [Company]'s work on [topic]. If another [role type] opens up, I'd love to be considered, and I'll keep following the team's progress.
:::

Prompt **P18** personalizes any of these from a profile you paste in. **P19** prepares you for the coffee chat.

## Coffee chats that turn into referrals

**Structure for a 20-minute call:**

1. **(2 min)** Thank them; give a 30-second intro (who you are, what you're targeting).
2. **(12 min)** Ask about *them and their team*: "What does a great first 90 days look like on your team?" · "What skills are hardest to hire for?" · "How does hiring usually work here: referrals, recruiters, postings?" · "What do you wish you'd known before joining?"
3. **(4 min)** Connect your experience to one thing they said.
4. **(2 min)** Ask: "Is there anyone else you'd suggest I talk to?" If there is an open role and the chat went well: "Would it be appropriate to mention our conversation when I apply?"
5. **Send the T13 thank-you within 24 hours.** If you apply, send T8 with a ready-to-paste summary.

**Do:** be on time, keep to time, take notes, and research their profile. **Don't:** ask for a job in the first minute, send your resume unasked, or "pick their brain" for an hour.

## Networking around real openings: the 3-touch sequence

For each high-priority posting:

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| **0** | Apply (tailored, Chapter 9). Send T1 to the recruiter and T3 to the hiring manager. |
| **1–2** | Message 1–2 people in the role (T5) or alumni at the company (T6). |
| **5–7** | Follow up once (T14) if there's no reply. |
| **After a chat** | Ask for a referral (T8) if appropriate. |
| **After an interview** | T15 within 24 hours; T16 if a promised date passes. |

## 7-day networking action plan

| Day | Goal | Actions (≈60–90 min/day) | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Profile ready | Run P08; update headline, About, skills; turn on Open to Work | Searchable profile |
| **2** | Target list | List 15 target companies; for each, find 1 recruiter, 1 hiring manager, 1 employee or alum | 45-person list in Workbook |
| **3** | Warm network | Send T7 to 10 former colleagues/managers; post a short "I'm looking for [role]" update | 10 messages + 1 post |
| **4** | Live openings | Find 5 postings from the last 72 hours at target companies; apply; send T1 + T3 for each | 5 applications, 10 messages |
| **5** | Informational chats | Send T5/T6 to 10 employees/alumni; book 2–3 chats | 10 messages |
| **6** | Founders & AI startups | Send T9/T10 to 5 founders or AI-startup ops leads; apply to 2 YC/Wellfound roles | 5 messages, 2 applications |
| **7** | Follow-up & review | Send T14 to non-responders from Days 3–4; run P20 on your pipeline; plan next week | Updated pipeline |

**Benchmarks:** response rates vary widely. Warm contacts and alumni typically reply far more often than cold recruiters. Track your own rates in the Workbook, and if a message type gets no replies after 15–20 sends, rewrite it.

::: {.five}
**Weekly target:** 25–30 personalized messages, 3 coffee chats, 5–10 tailored applications.

**Best contacts:** former colleagues, alumni, people currently in the role, and the recruiter on a fresh posting.

**Always:** one specific detail, one easy ask, one polite follow-up.
:::
